How your plan works.

Your coverage is a package of fixed-benefit policies. Each one pays a set dollar amount when a covered event happens — a doctor visit, a prescription, an ER trip, a night in the hospital — and pays it to you, from the first dollar. Your policy schedule (in the portal) lists the exact amounts and how many visits per year your plan includes.

Because each policy pays on its own, one event can trigger several at once. A hospital stay pays the admission benefit, the daily benefit, your hospital indemnity lump sum, and your gap benefit, all separately.

In network, it works like regular insurance. Hand over your ID card, the provider bills ManhattanLife, and if the benefit is more than the bill, the difference comes to you.

Out of network, it pays exactly the same. You'll schedule as a self-pay patient (ask for the self-pay discount), pay, and upload the receipt for reimbursement. In-network is easier and cheaper, but you're never stuck.

What this plan is not. It isn't major medical and it isn't an ACA plan. There's no annual out-of-pocket maximum, so a long hospitalization or a major diagnosis can cost more than the benefits pay. That's exactly why the $2,500 rule exists — CareGuide routinely cuts hospital bills by 40–50% — and why we review your coverage together every year.

Prescriptions.

Your plan reimburses $50 per person per day you fill a prescription, up to the annual maximum on your schedule. Use any discount card you like, pay the discounted price at the counter, then photograph the receipt and upload it in the Easy Upload app. If your prescriptions cost less than $50 that day, you keep the difference.

Prices for the same drug vary wildly between pharmacies. Check before you fill — the cheapest pharmacy means the biggest reimbursement in your pocket.
